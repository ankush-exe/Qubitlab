# Superposition explained

A qubit state is a weighted combination of the basis states $|0>$ and $|1>$: $|psi> = alpha|0> + beta|1>$. The complex amplitudes satisfy $|alpha|^2 + |beta|^2 = 1$.

Superposition does not mean that a hidden classical bit is randomly switching. It means the state has amplitudes for both measurement outcomes. A measurement samples one outcome, with probabilities given by the squared magnitudes of those amplitudes.

Apply H to $|0>$ and both outcomes have probability one half. Apply H again and the two paths interfere, returning the qubit to $|0>$. The second operation is a useful reminder that amplitudes, not just probabilities, carry information.
